# 16-Week Running + Gym Guide

## Targets

1. Rebuild consistent running and move the Garmin running VO₂-max estimate from 55.9 toward 60.
2. Establish the aerobic base and speed endurance needed to pursue a sub-9:00 3 km afterward.

You are 22, returning from essentially no current running, with historical PBs of 48.6 for 400 m and 1:53 for 800 m. That speed is useful, but your tendons, calves, feet and aerobic durability are currently deconditioned. This plan therefore uses frequent easy movement, two gym sessions, and only one hard running session at first.

## Heart-rate rules

Your recorded 192 bpm is an unverified watch reading, not a tested maximum. Until you perform a supervised or well-controlled field test, use these as **provisional ceilings**, not medical limits:

| Session | Provisional maximum | How it should feel |
|---|---:|---|
| Rest / mobility | 120 bpm | Completely easy |
| Recovery jog | 135 bpm | Very relaxed |
| Easy aerobic run | 145 bpm | Full-sentence conversation |
| Long easy run | 150 bpm | Comfortable; slow down in heat/hills |
| Steady aerobic | 160 bpm | Controlled, never straining |
| Threshold / tempo | 175 bpm | Hard but repeatable; RPE 7–8/10 |
| VO₂ intervals | 185 bpm | RPE 8–9/10; HR rises late in each rep |
| Strides / sprint drills | 175 bpm | Fast and relaxed; stop before straining |
| Gym strength | 145 bpm | Keep lifting quality high |

For every workout, the HR ceiling is secondary to symptoms and effort. Stop for chest pain, faintness, unusual breathlessness, palpitations or sharp pain. Do not force a target HR in Brisbane heat. A chest strap is strongly preferred.

Garmin generally needs an outdoor GPS run with HR above 70% of maximum for at least 10 continuous minutes to calculate a running estimate. Use a chest strap and a correctly configured maximum HR when possible.

## Weekly structure

- Monday: gym strength A + optional mobility
- Tuesday: quality running
- Wednesday: easy running
- Thursday: gym strength B + optional recovery jog only if fresh
- Friday: easy running or recovery
- Saturday: rest / mobility
- Sunday: long easy run

Do not turn the optional sessions into extra hard sessions. Keep at least 48 hours between demanding running workouts.

## Gym sessions

### Strength A — Monday

1. Back squat or leg press: prescribed sets × 5–8 reps
2. Romanian deadlift: same sets × 6–8
3. Split squat: 2–3 × 8 each leg
4. Standing calf raise: 3 × 10–15
5. Tibialis raise: 2–3 × 12–20
6. Plank or dead bug: 3 sets

### Strength B — Thursday

1. Trap-bar deadlift or deadlift: prescribed sets × 4–6 reps
2. Step-up: 2–3 × 8 each leg
3. Hip thrust: 2–3 × 8–10
4. Seated soleus raise: 3 × 10–15
5. Hamstring curl: 2–3 × 8–12
6. Pull-up/row and press: 2–3 × 6–10 each

Keep 1–3 repetitions in reserve. No failure lifting, maximal plyometrics or heavy leg work the day before Tuesday intervals. Add low-volume plyometrics only from week 9 if calves and Achilles are completely symptom-free: 2×10 pogo hops and 3×20 m relaxed bounds after the warm-up.

## Warm-up and cool-down

Before quality sessions: 12–15 minutes easy jog, dynamic mobility, then 3–4 progressive 60–80 m strides. After: 10 minutes easy. Before every run, check that walking and the first five minutes are pain-free.

## Progress checks

- At the end of weeks 4, 8 and 12, review soreness, sleep, resting HR and whether easy pace is improving at the same HR.
- Re-test a controlled 3 km only in week 16, not repeatedly.
- Use Garmin outdoor runs of at least 20 minutes with reliable HR to encourage an updated estimate.
- If fatigue, resting HR or soreness is clearly elevated for 2–3 days, remove the next quality session and run easy instead.

## Daily nutrition and body-mass target

At 191 cm and 75 kg, your BMI is approximately 20.6. Do not diet down while trying to rebuild aerobic fitness. Start with this practical intake trial for 14 days:

| Day | Starting calories | Carbohydrate | Protein | Fat |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Rest / mobility | 2,800–3,000 kcal | 300–375 g | 125–140 g | 80–100 g |
| Easy run or gym | 3,000–3,200 kcal | 350–425 g | 125–140 g | 80–100 g |
| VO₂, threshold or long run | 3,200–3,500 kcal | 425–525 g | 125–140 g | 80–100 g |

These are starting estimates, not a metabolic test. Weigh yourself three mornings per week and use the weekly average. For an athletic build, aim to gain roughly **0.1–0.25 kg per week**. If weight is flat for two weeks and training feels under-fuelled, add 150–200 kcal/day. If you gain more than 0.4 kg/week for two weeks, remove 100–150 kcal/day. Keep protein spread over 4–5 feedings of roughly 25–35 g; AIS guidance places heavy-training protein around 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day and emphasizes spreading it across the day. [AIS protein guidance](https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/supplements/group_a/sports-foods2/isolated-protein-supplement2/what-is-it)

### Simple meal timing

- 1–3 hours before a hard or long session: 75–120 g carbohydrate plus 20–30 g protein. Examples: oats with banana and yoghurt; rice and eggs; toast, honey and milk.
- During sessions under 60 minutes: water is usually enough. For sessions over 60–75 minutes, take 30–60 g carbohydrate per hour and drink to thirst.
- Within two hours after hard training: 25–35 g protein plus 75–90 g carbohydrate. Add salt and fluids if you sweated heavily.
- Before bed: 25–35 g protein, such as Greek yoghurt, milk, cottage cheese or a casein shake.

Do not chronically train fasted during this 16-week block. Low energy availability will undermine recovery, immune function, iron status and the VO₂ goal.

## Iron-specific plan

Ferritin around 9 should be treated as a clinical issue, not simply a genetic quirk or a normal training response. Before progressing into the hardest weeks, arrange a GP or sports-physician review and ask what monitoring schedule is appropriate. Useful tests commonly include a CBC, ferritin, transferrin saturation, serum iron and an inflammation marker; the AIS/IOC guidance emphasizes multiple iron markers and clinical follow-up. [AIS iron best practice](https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/position_statements/iron-deficiency), [IOC supplement consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29540367/)

Until that review:

- Keep the clinician-prescribed tablet and dose exactly as directed; do not add extra iron or stack multivitamins containing iron.
- Eat one iron-rich food daily: lean beef or lamb, mussels/oysters, sardines, eggs, tofu, lentils, beans, iron-fortified cereal or dark leafy greens.
- Pair plant iron with vitamin C: lentils with tomatoes, beans with capsicum, or fortified cereal with berries/kiwifruit.
- Keep tea, coffee, calcium supplements and large dairy servings roughly 1–2 hours away from an iron tablet or your main iron-rich meal if your clinician agrees.
- Recheck bloods on the schedule your clinician sets; do not wait until performance collapses.
- If you develop unusual breathlessness, palpitations, dizziness, faintness, chest pain, marked fatigue or worsening exercise tolerance, stop hard training and seek medical care.

The adult male dietary reference intake is 8 mg/day, but that number is not a treatment target for recurrent deficiency. High-dose iron can cause harm; the NIH lists 45 mg/day as the adult tolerable upper intake level for routine intake, while medically supervised treatment is a separate matter. [NIH iron fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/)

## Stretching, mobility and tissue care

Before every run, do 8–12 minutes of dynamic preparation:

1. 5–8 minutes brisk walk/easy jog.
2. 10 ankle rocks per side.
3. 10 leg swings front/back and side-to-side per leg.
4. 2×20 m marching, skips or relaxed drills.
5. 3–4 progressive strides before quality sessions.

After easy runs, do 5–8 minutes easy walking, then 20–30 seconds each of calf, hip-flexor, hamstring and glute stretches. Keep post-run stretching gentle; it should not create pain or next-day soreness.

Three times weekly, preferably after gym or easy running, add:

- Bent-knee soleus calf raises: 3×12–15
- Straight-knee calf raises: 3×12–15
- Tibialis raises: 2×15–20
- Short-foot/foot doming: 2×10
- Copenhagen plank or side plank: 2×20–30 seconds each side

Use a foam roller only for comfort; it is not a substitute for sleep, food or load management.

## Sleep, hydration and recovery

- Target 8–9 hours in bed, with a consistent wake time.
- Take one genuinely low-load day each week; walking and mobility are fine.
- Baseline fluid target: roughly 2.3–2.6 L/day, then add fluid for heat and sweat. Do not force water until urine is perfectly clear.
- For sessions longer than an hour or very hot Brisbane workouts, use a drink containing carbohydrate and sodium rather than water alone. Practice your strategy in training.
- Keep alcohol minimal, especially after intervals or long runs.
- Record morning body weight, sleep duration, resting HR and a 1–10 fatigue score. If resting HR is unusually elevated, sleep is poor, or legs feel heavy for two consecutive days, replace the next hard workout with easy running or rest.

## Optional performance supplements

Creatine monohydrate 3–5 g/day is reasonable for the gym/athletic-build goal if your clinician has no reason to avoid it; expect possible water-weight gain. Caffeine can help selected hard sessions, but do not use it to override fatigue and avoid it within 8–10 hours of bedtime. Neither replaces carbohydrate, iron management, sleep or a proper training progression.
